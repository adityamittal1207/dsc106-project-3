Wildfire across the West, hour by hour

Exploring what a geostationary satellite sees of a fire season: where the fires were, how much power they put out, what time of day they burn, and how far they run.

1

Where was the fire at its peak hour?

Every fire pixel the satellite saw during the hour of highest power in the selected window, drawn over all the ground that burned earlier in that window. Each shape is one satellite pixel at its true footprint, about 2 by 3 km here. On the regional map, burning pixels are enlarged so they stay visible.

Hover a pixel for its power. Choose a fire above to zoom in on its final perimeter.

2

How many fire pixels did the satellite see each hour?

Average fire pixels per scan in each solar hour. A count measures how much ground was burning hot enough to detect, not how hard it burned; graph 4 shows the power.

Click or drag across the chart to move the map in graph 3 to that hour.

3

Watch the fires move, hour by hour

The map from graph 1 at any hour. Ground that burned earlier in the window stays grey, so the active front stands out from the scar it leaves behind.

Arrow keys step one hour once the slider has focus. Busiest 3 days in graph 4 narrows the slider to a single run.

4

How much power were the fires emitting?

Total fire radiative power, averaged over the scans in each hour. Power is a rate, so scans are averaged rather than summed. Single-scan false alarms are removed.

Drag across the lower strip to zoom. Graphs 1, 2, 3 and 5 follow the same window.
